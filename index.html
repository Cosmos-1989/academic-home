Zhenyu Sun

I am an Assistant Professor in the School of Mathematics and Statistics at Northeastern University at Qinhuangdao.

As an undergraduate and graduate student, I studied physics, philosophy, and mathematical logic.

I also participated in a graduate program in “High-Tech Entrepreneurship” and led several software development and high-performance computing projects.

Research

Logic, Foundations, and Mathematical AI

I study mathematical logic and the foundations of mathematics. I also develop agentic AI systems for mathematical research and formal proof systems, with Lean as the primary platform for formalization.

Computational Physics and Neuroscience

My interests in computational physics and computational neuroscience include plasma modeling, connectomics and whole-brain modeling, high-throughput signal processing, and the development and verification of cyber-physical systems.

Quantitative Finance and Computing

I work on factor engineering, algorithmic trading, and market microstructure, as well as high-performance computing systems for crypto assets and modern financial derivatives.

Other Mathematical and Scientific Interests

I also explore other questions in mathematics and the natural sciences.

Topology

Undergraduate general topology. 20 lectures of 90 minutes each (30 hours).

Topological spaces and continuous maps; subspaces, products, and quotients; separation, countability, connectedness, and compactness; continuous functions and metrization.

Prerequisites. Mathematical analysis, including completeness of the real numbers, sequences and series, continuity in Euclidean spaces, and basic proof methods. No prior knowledge of category theory or abstract algebra is required. Concrete constructions are accompanied by their mapping properties.

Reading. Zhenyu Sun, Topology Lecture Notes, Volume I, General Topology and the Theory of Spaces (2026 revision, in Chinese). Chapter and section references below refer to these notes.

Slides are in Chinese. Lectures 1–15 are available in the red-theme edition; Lecture 3 uses the expanded version. Later lectures will be added as they are completed. Class dates will be announced separately.

Functional Analysis

Undergraduate course. 20 lectures of 90 minutes each (30 hours).

Approximation and convergence, existence and stability of solutions, and the spectral analysis of operators. Examples from quantum mechanics, Fourier analysis, and integral equations motivate the theory.

Prerequisites. Mathematical analysis, linear algebra, and basic metric-space topology. The necessary background on Lebesgue integration and Lp spaces is introduced as supplementary material.

Slides are in Chinese. Lectures 1–6 are available below; later lectures will be added as they are completed.
